# Direction C, Targeting: critique by round

Judged against the spec's goals and anti-goals, CORANTO's bar and the five Review Focus lines, on the six stills from `tools/redesign-brief.ts stills` (port 4789, a scratch home, the GPU lock held).

What C is: the Brief as a HUD laid over the live city, not a pane of glass. A warm black panel with no edge but its marks (four amber corner brackets and a range rail down its right side), Big Shoulders (a condensed display face cut for Chicago's signage) for the date, the headlines and the keys, Azeret Mono for every figure. Amber is the voice, a hot red is kept for the lock and for granting, and one cold ice is for looking and for time. The city pane gets its own frame: corner brackets, a heading tape, a sweep, and telemetry. Each waiting item with a district is numbered (01 to 06) and pinned over its tower; the active item (hovered, focused, or else the first) gets a lock-on round its tower and a leader line from its row. Behind the list runs a stream of the brief's own names and figures. Copy is cut as B cut it (B's `brief-words.ts`, reused): a row is a meta line, a headline and readouts, and the sentence waits under Why.

## Round 1

What works:
- It is unmistakably not A or B: no box, no glass, no green or cyan. The city reads as the subject and the panel as the instrument pointed at it.
- The lock-on is the idea paying off: harbor-ledger's tower is bracketed root to tip in red, with its number, name and "grant? 48/50" beside it, while the same red frames the ask in the list. The list and the city say the same thing.
- The grant key is the only hot, filled object on the screen, in a breathing red reticle with a turning seal. Review Focus 4 is met at a glance.
- Big Shoulders in caps for the date and in mixed case for headlines reads like an instrument's labelling, not a newspaper and not a game.
- The rangefinders (a ruled scale, a lit run, the cap as a red bracket) carry the usage sentence in two lines.
- Over the overview (Focus 2) every word on the panel holds; the panel is dark enough under its words without any blur.

Weaknesses:
1. **The lock's tag runs off the window** at the right in brief, brief-autonomy and brief-1280: "HARBOR-LEDG", "grant? 48/5". The direction's centrepiece is clipped. Anti-goal: low quality.
2. **The sweep is caught mid-pass as a broad ice pillar** (brief-overview, brief-1280): a 160 px band that reads as a light beam or a glitch standing in the city, not a scan. Anti-goal: kitsch, and it fights the city.
3. **The leader line is too faint, and wrong when its row has scrolled away.** A 1 px dotted line that crosses the assay-kit pin on its way; in brief-scrolled it leaves from the panel's top for a row no longer on screen.
4. **Pins collide**: assay-kit's and kumo's stand on neighbouring towers and their tags overlap ("60 cr" under "05 KUMO").
5. **The autonomy strip says something false.** The last ten ticks are lit as "here" and the two misses fall inside them, so the strip reads "missed 2 of the last 10 here" beside the words "10/10". The run here and the run overall need their own scales.
6. **Empty designators read as checkboxes** in the other ledgers (brief-scrolled): a small bracketed square before each merged row looks like something to tick.
7. **Still a strip of keys**: a proposal row carries four reticles (Why, Tier, Dismiss, Post), and Why and Tier sit in the same tick frames as Dismiss. Looking should be lighter than any framed key.
8. **The stream is legible behind the autonomy ask**: whole letters stand in columns under the strip and the keys, which is noise exactly where the heaviest decision sits.

## Round 2

Changes from round 1: the lock's tag stands left of its brackets when it would pass the window's edge; the sweep is a hairline with a short trail; the leader is a solid line with a soft glow, and it waits while its row is scrolled out of the list; a pin whose tag would overlap one already placed is lifted above it on a stepped leader; the autonomy ask shows two scales (the master's last 50 over every repo, the misses red at the end, and its last 10 here) so they no longer contradict; empty designators are a short hairline, not a box; looking keys are a cold word whose reticle appears only when aimed at; the ask's fill is solid enough that the stream recedes behind it.

What works now:
- The lock reads whole in every still, its tag inside the window, flipped at 1280 and at 1600.
- The proposal rows sort at a glance: an ice word (Why), two framed amber keys (Tier, Dismiss), then the lit Post key with its sum. Looking no longer looks like a control you might hit by accident.
- The ask's evidence is true to the line it parses and reads in one look: a long amber run with two red ticks, and a short bright run of ten.
- The pins clear one another (kumo stands above assay-kit on a stepped leader).

Weaknesses:
1. **The leader is a laser across the city.** Leaving the row level, it runs the whole pane at one height, through assay-kit's tower and under its pin, to the side of harbor-ledger's. It should climb to the target's head, where the ring is, not cut the skyline in half.
2. **The panel is one flat slab.** Brackets and a rail, but the head, the list and the ask sit on the same brown surface. The spec asks for layered panels with shading; B has three depths, C has one. Anti-goal: no depth, layering or shading.
3. **The audit section's note rags**: "Nothing runs until you / post." leaves one word on its own line.
4. **"here 10/10" wraps** under its short scale, so the label stands on two lines.
5. **The sweep still reads as a stray line** when a still catches it mid-pane (brief-1280): a full-height ice rule standing beside harbor-ledger.

## Round 3

Changes from round 2: the leader leaves the rail level for a short run, then climbs straight to the ring at the target's head, ending on the ring's edge; the head is its own plate (darker, lit along its top, a fine graticule behind the rangefinders, a shadow cast down onto the list); the panel is shaded top to foot and carries a faint machined inner frame 6 px inside its brackets; the active row casts a shadow; the ask is raised on a deep shadow with a lit top edge; the audit note sets as one balanced line; the ask's labels hold to one line; the sweep is fainter.

What works now:
- Three depths read in a still: the head plate over the list, the red ask raised over its section, and the HUD over the city with its own frame, pins and lock.
- The leader now says "this row is that tower": it climbs from the ask to harbor-ledger's tip, where the red ring turns, and the tower is bracketed root to tip.
- Over the overview (Focus 2), the climb is short and clean, the lock tag sits on dark ground beside the brightest tower, and the panel's words hold.
- The scrolled end (Focus 1) stays inside the brackets to Today's limit, and the leader is gone while no waiting row is on screen.

Weaknesses:
1. **The flipped lock tag is crossed by the leader** (brief, brief-1280): flipped left of the brackets at the ring's height, it stands exactly where the climbing line arrives, so the line ends under the tag's corner.
2. **At 1280 the leader crosses assay-kit's mark** on its way up. A crossing is sometimes unavoidable; it should at least pass under the pins, not through their marks.
3. **The stream's glyphs repeat in patterns** ("A I A I A"): one short word sampled at a fixed stride. It reads as a texture of letters, not as the machine reading its own figures.
4. **The sweep still stands as a full-height line** when a still catches it mid-pane. It needs ends that say "scan cursor", not "stray rule".
5. **Two languages in the city**: the pins (Big Shoulders, amber) stand over the city's own plates (Newsreader, cream) and repeat the district names. The plates are the city's and out of scope here, but if C is picked Stage 2 must settle which one names a tower.

## Round 4

Changes from round 3: a flipped lock tag stands up and to the left of the ring, clear of the leader climbing in from below; the stream reads the brief's own names and figures downward word by word (HARBOR-LEDGER, 60CR, #312, HALTED), a dot between; the sweep is a scan cursor, a bright tick at the tape and at the foot joined by a faint hairline.

What works now:
- At 1600 the lock tag stands above harbor-ledger's tip and the leader arrives at the ring untouched.
- The sweep reads as an instrument's cursor in any still, its two ticks tied to the frame.
- The stream, in a crop, is words of this morning, not a font sample.

Weaknesses:
1. **At 1280 the flipped lock tag lands on the pane's telemetry** ("LOCK 01 HARBOR-LEDGER" at the top right), and the two say the same thing: the telemetry's lock line is a repeat.
2. **The leader shows through the pin tags** it passes under (assay-kit at 1280): their ground is too thin to cover it.
3. **In brief-autonomy the ask's top brackets fade** into the list's 22 px top fade, so the lock's frame looks cut at its head.

None of these is at the anti-goal level; they are placement faults, fixed in round 5.

## Round 5 (final)

Changes from round 4: the telemetry keeps one line (track 06) and moves to the pane's top left, the lock's tag being the only place the lock is named; pin tags stand on denser ground, so a leader passing under them stops at their edges; the list's top fade is 12 px and the ask stands 16 px down, so its brackets show whole.

Weakness found at the test gate, at the anti-goal level: **tracked capitals.** The date, the keys, the labels, the pins and the telemetry were all set in letterspaced caps, and the repo's own test (`test/no-tracked-caps.test.ts`, the first item of Connor's no-AI-look list) refuses it. A "Brief" kicker also stood over the day, against the rule that the day is the heading.

## Round 6

Changes from round 5: every caps setting is gone; the date, headlines, keys and labels are sentence case, the tracking cut to at most 0.04 em; the since line moves under the day as an ice readout, and nothing stands over the day. The final stills below are this round's.

What round 6 shows: the condensed face carries the HUD without caps. "Friday 20 November" in Big Shoulders reads as an instrument's own lettering rather than a game's; "Let it act here" in sentence case on the hot key is calmer and still the heaviest object on the screen; the pins read "harbor-ledger", "kumo" as the repos are named. No anti-goal-level weakness remains.

What the final stills show:
- At 1280 (Focus 5) the lock tag stands clear above harbor-ledger, the telemetry clear at the pane's left, and nothing in the panel clips: the keys, the sums and the grant key sit whole.
- In brief-autonomy (Focus 4) the ask stands first, bracketed in red top and foot, with the only hot filled key on the screen, and the same red lock stands on its tower in the city at the end of a red leader. Nothing else on either side of the screen is red and filled.
- With reduced motion (Focus 3) the page is whole at 0.7 s: no scan, no sweep, no stream fall, no breathing reticle and no turning seal; the lock, pins and leader stand at rest where they belong.

Against the anti-goals, nothing left at that level:
- Low quality: every mark is placed by the city's own projection and clears the window's edges and its neighbours.
- Boring fonts: Big Shoulders (sentence case throughout, no tracked capitals) with Azeret Mono for every figure. Plex and Newsreader are gone from the Brief.
- Boring buttons: four weights built differently. Looking is a cold word whose reticle appears when aimed at; deciding sits in plain amber corner ticks; spending is a lit amber key in a heavier reticle with its sum in its own window; granting is the one hot red key, a breathing reticle and a turning seal.
- Boring colours: warm black, amber as the voice, red kept for lock and grant, ice for looking and time, mint for merged.
- No depth: the head plate casts on the list; the ask is raised on its own shadow; the HUD stands over the city in its own frame; the lock reaches into the city.
- Simple code: the rangefinders, the two agreement scales, the claims bar, the pins and the lock carry what the sentences used to.
- Retro: no grain, scanlines or CRT blur. The stream is the brief's own words and stays under 20% opacity.

What remains, below the anti-goal level, for Stage 2 if this direction is picked:
1. The city's own plates (Newsreader) and the HUD's pins name the same towers in two voices. Stage 2 should pick one.
2. A leader may still pass under a pin on its way to the lock; the pin covers it, but a routed leader would be cleaner.
3. Readouts parse the engine's sentences (B's `brief-words.ts`, reused). Stage 2 should give the Brief these as fields.
4. Items with no district (Today's limit, the week's tour) get no pin; their place in the HUD is only the list.
5. The motion (the scan on open, the sweep, the closing brackets, the stream) never shows in a still; the pick sheet should say so.

The Brief open is within 1.3% of main's on high and equal on medium, well inside the 10% gate. The city-alone rows sit within run-to-run noise of every baseline run: the HUD's frame hook is registered only while the Brief is open, and its layer is display: none otherwise.

What keeps it cheap: no backdrop-filter anywhere; the frame hook projects at most one point pair per district (six here) and writes transforms, plus one SVG path's points; a tower's frame is re-read every 90 frames, not every frame; the active row's position is read only when the list scrolls or the lock moves, never in a frame that wrote; the stream is 18 columns of text, each a composited layer moved by transform; the sweep, the ring and the seal move by transform; the glows are static shadows.

# Continuing C after the pick: four changes

Connor picked C and asked for four changes before the design is locked: one set of labels in the city, B's depth, the running head in C's language, and leaders that never cross another pin. Same harness, a fresh scratch home (port 4789, the GPU lock held), seven stills now: the six plus `brief-closed.png`.

**The choice on labels.** While the Brief is open the city's own plates (Newsreader, cream) fade out over 0.45 s and the HUD names the towers alone. A district with something waiting keeps its numbered amber pin; **a repo district with nothing waiting gets a quiet pin** (a 6 px tick and its name, dim, on a thin ground, never numbered, never lifted, and it waits rather than cover a target's tag). The firms' quarters and the exchange are not districts and get no pin: while the Brief is open the city is read as the brief's targets and where the rest of the repos stand, and the firms' standings are another screen's. Quiet pins beat hiding because, with the plates gone, a tower with nothing on it would otherwise be nameless, and the lock reads stronger against named neighbours than against blanks. When the Brief closes the plates come back (`brief-closed.png`).

## Round 7

Changes: the plates handoff and quiet pins; a back pane behind the panel, the head made a nearer plate (proud of the panel at the left, lit along its top and left edges, a long shadow down and right), hover parallax on the head and the back pane by transform only; the running head in Big Shoulders, the current place in amber corner ticks, Find as a cold key with its shortcut in its own window, a ruled tape under the head; the leader routed in right angles through clear channels, every pin, its stem and the lock's tag treated as boxes to keep 7 px clear of.

What works:
- One voice in the city. "quarry" and "blank-slate" stand as quiet pins beside the numbered ones; nothing in Newsreader is left over the HUD.
- The head reads as the nearest plate: it overhangs the panel's left bracket and casts onto the list.
- The running head no longer looks like a book's title line: "Palimpsest" in the condensed face, a divider tick, the places, the current one held in ticks exactly as a target is held in the list.

Weaknesses:
1. **The leader still cuts the skyline.** With no cost on the way it arrives, it came in level at the ring's height (brief) or climbed the target tower's own face from below (overview, 1280), drawn across lit glass.
2. **The back pane reads as a stray strip** beside the rail, too narrow and too dim to say "pane".

## Round 8

Changes: the router prefers arriving from above (level costs more, from below most), so the line climbs at the pane's edge, crosses the sky and comes down onto the ring; the back pane stands wider and lower, lit on its right and foot, the panel casting onto it.

What works: in every still the leader leaves the row, runs up the frame's edge, crosses the sky above every tag and drops onto the ring; it crosses no pin, no stem and no tag.

Weaknesses:
1. **The channel grazes the telemetry** ("track 06" at the frame's top left) in brief-overview and brief-1280, the line running under its letters.
2. **The stub column sits on the back pane's lit edge**, two parallel lines a pixel apart.

## Round 9 (final)

Changes: the telemetry moves to the frame's foot and is itself a box the router avoids; the stub column stands 44 px out from the rail, clear of the back pane's edge.

What the final stills show:
- brief, brief-autonomy, brief-1280 and brief-overview: the leader in three or four right-angled runs with cut corners, clear of kumo's and assay-kit's stepped tags, of the lock's flipped tag and of the telemetry; nothing in the city is crossed.
- brief-closed: the plates back in Newsreader under the running head, which now marks City in its ticks.
- brief-scrolled: rows slide under the head plate, its shadow falling on them; the leader is gone while no waiting row is on screen.
- brief-reduced-motion: no parallax, no transition on the plates or panes; everything at rest.

The parallax never shows in a still (the harness parks the pointer outside the panel). Over the panel it moves the head up to 5 px with the pointer and the back pane up to 7 px against it, transform only, one write a frame at most.

What remains for Stage 2:
1. Only one leader is drawn at a time, so the rule "offset two lines that would cross" applies to the leader against the pins' own stepped stems (each stem is part of its pin's box). If Stage 2 draws a leader per visible row, the router should take the leaders already placed as boxes too.
2. The firms and the exchange are unnamed while the Brief is open; if that is missed, they can join as quiet pins.
3. The running head's new face holds 4.5:1 over lit glass (the scrim test reads it), but the other screens still use Plex and Newsreader under it. Stage 2 should carry the language down into them.
