# Direction A, Phosphor: critique by round

Judged against the spec's goals and anti-goals, CORANTO's bar and the five Review Focus lines, on the six stills from `tools/redesign-brief.ts stills`.

## Round 1

What it is: the Brief as a floating phosphor terminal inset from the window, Martian Mono throughout (its width axis gives the date its wide, heavy display cut), a readout strip in the pinned head (waiting, merged, credits spent, the two windows as segmented meters with the cap marked), Waiting on you as a recessed inner screen with a breathing mark, sentences that type in on open, a scan band on open, and actions as bracketed keys in four weights.

What works:
- The date and the readouts already feel like an instrument, not a newspaper. The figures replace three lines of prose.
- The autonomy ask is clearly the heaviest thing on its screen: a raised, lit panel and a solid lit key.
- Over the overview (Review Focus 2) every line holds; the glass with its backdrop dim keeps the brightest towers out of the text.
- At 1280 x 800 (Review Focus 5) nothing clips; the meters move beside the figures.

Weaknesses:
1. **Depth is claimed, not shown.** The terminal behind shows only as a thin sliver at the right edge and reads like a rendering fault. The panel is one dark slab with a 1px edge; no layer reads as nearer or farther except the autonomy panel. Anti-goal: flat panels.
2. **Every row is a strip of boxes.** Four outlined keys per row (Post as a job, Change the tier, Dismiss, Ask why) make a dashboard-kit grid. Spend, decide and look differ only in fill strength, so at a glance Dismiss weighs as much as Post as a job. Anti-goal: boring buttons, "done in simple code".
3. **Still too much text.** Every row is a chip, a two or three line sentence and a key row. The green dateline chip repeats twenty times and becomes noise. Lines stop at 64ch with a wide dead margin on the right, so sentences wrap more than they need to.
4. **The running head's backing ends in a hard dark rectangle** over the sky at the top, a visible seam.
5. **No machined detail.** The frame is a plain rounded rectangle; nothing says made object: no bezel, no registration, no light catching an edge. Against CORANTO it looks styled, not built.
6. **The since line vanished** into the screen-reader text; when the Brief covers from is a fact worth one readout.
7. **The Recommended section's audit note** is a four-line paragraph in the middle of the list (Review Focus 1: the scrolled end reads as prose again).
8. **Reduced motion** (Review Focus 3) reads whole, which passes, but it looks identical to the animated still because nothing in the still shows the motion's resting state; fine, but the cursor and scan must never leave a residue.

## Round 2

Changes from round 1: the two mornings before stand behind this one as older screens, set back and to the right, each with its date up its visible edge (a palimpsest of briefs, and real depth that the glass cannot show through); a machined frame with lit corner registrations and light caught on the top edge; a meta line over each headline (repo lit, time dim, a state tag at the right: proposal, review, merged, failed, halted, limit, authority) in place of the green chip; keys in four clearly different weights (look is bare words that close into jaws under the pointer, decide is unlit jaws, spend is a lit keycap with a lip of shadow, grant is the solid lit key with its seal); since as a readout; the audit note cut to one line; Handled today compacted to one line of keys; the running head's backing runs the window's width with no seam; a narrow window shrinks the meters and drops the since figure.

What works now:
- The stack of mornings is the first thing that reads as depth, and it means something: yesterday's and the day before's briefs, set back.
- At a glance the keys sort themselves: lit keycaps are money, jaws are decisions, bare words are looking. Rows no longer read as a strip of boxes.
- 1280 x 800 holds: the meters stay inside the head.

Weaknesses:
1. **The autonomy ask still argues in a paragraph.** Its evidence (48 of the last 50 agreed, the last 10 here) is the reason to grant, and it is buried mid-sentence. It should be an instrument: an agreement strip he can read in a second. Review Focus 4.
2. **The grant key reads as a generic green call-to-action slab.** Heaviest, yes, but not crafted: one gradient, a small seal. It needs the build of a physical key: a recessed seal compartment, a bevel, a lit edge.
3. **Tags compete with keys.** Every proposal row carries a lit green tag the same brightness as the spend keycaps, so the eye lands on "proposal" four times before it lands on Post as a job. Lit should mean act.
4. **The glass has no edge light.** The panel's outline is a line; nothing blooms where the phosphor meets the dark city, so it sits on the city rather than glowing over it. Over the overview the panel and the stack read slightly as cut-outs.
5. **The meta line's repo and the headline sit too close in weight to the sentence**; the sentence block (12px, 340) is still the biggest mass on every row. The headline should carry more and the sentence less.
6. **The waiting recess's left edge** leaves a dark gutter between the frame and the recess that reads as an accident at full size.

## Round 3

Changes from round 2: the autonomy ask carries its evidence as an instrument (48 of 50 agreed, one cell a decision, the two misses outlined in amber, beside its run here); the grant key rebuilt as a guarded key (a dark guard ring and a lit outer ring round a bevelled lit key, a recessed seal set into its left end); state tags drop to outlines so only keys are lit, except the autonomy ask's tag, which is solid; the panel's edge blooms where the green meets the city; Waiting on you is a recessed band across the panel's full width, so no gutter shows; headlines heavier and sentences smaller and dimmer.

What works now:
- Review Focus 4 is met with room to spare: the autonomy ask is the brightest, deepest, most built object on the page, and the agreement strip makes the case in one look.
- The page reads in three levels at arm's length: the date and its readouts, the lit keycaps, then the headlines.
- Review Focus 1: the scrolled end stays a clean list to its last row (Today's limit and its key), inside the frame.

Weaknesses:
1. **The grant key's seal reads as an X.** Two crossed bars in a dark square is the universal close glyph, on the one key that grants authority. A quality fault at the anti-goal level; it must be a lit seal, not a cross.
2. **A meta line with no time** (Recommended's fieldnotes) sets the repo in the dim time style, indented; a row with no dateline (Today's limit) puts its tag at the left instead of the right.
3. **The autonomy ask still repeats its evidence in prose** under the strip. The words are the engine's and stay in this prototype, but Stage 3 should cut the first sentence once the strip carries it.
4. **The opening scan and type-in are never seen in a still.** They are built and they respect reduced motion (the reduced-motion still is whole at 0.7 s, with no cursor), but Connor will judge motion he cannot see; the pick sheet should say so.

## Round 4

Changes from round 3: the grant key's seal is a lit core in a lit ring set in a dark well, an indicator that reads as on, not as close; a meta line with no time sets the repo lit, and the state tag always stands at the right.

Against the anti-goals, nothing left at that level:
- Low quality: every object is built (bevels, lips, guard ring, recessed bands, lit cells); no default control remains on the Brief.
- Boring fonts: one family, Martian Mono, used across its width axis, wide and heavy for the date, condensed for readouts, normal for reading.
- Boring buttons: four weights by consequence, each built differently.
- Boring colours: one phosphor green on black glass, with amber and red kept for state alone. This is the direction's own brief, and the risk is named below.
- No depth: the stack of earlier mornings behind, the recessed waiting band, the raised autonomy panel, keycaps above the glass.
- Simple code: the agreement strip, the meters and the stack carry information, not decoration.
- Retro: no grain, scanlines or CRT blur; the scan is one clean band on open.

Review Focus, all five: the scrolled end is clean to its last row (1); the overview's lit towers never reach the text through the dimmed glass (2); with reduced motion the page is whole at 0.7 s with no cursor and no scan (3); the autonomy ask is the heaviest object (4); at 1280 x 800 the head and keys fit (5).

What remains, below the anti-goal level, for Stage 2 if this direction is picked:
1. The autonomy ask's first sentence repeats the agreement strip; cut it in Stage 3's copy pass.
2. The readouts parse the merged count and credits from the engine's lead sentence; Stage 2 should give the Brief those numbers as fields.
3. Mono for every sentence is dense over a long Brief. It holds here at 11.5px, but run output and the terminal mode will test it harder.
4. The motion (scan on open, type-in, the breathing waiting mark) is never seen in a still.
5. Every row in Waiting on you repeats the same three keys; a long morning of proposals will read as a column of identical keycaps.

At 60 Hz every view holds 16.70 ms, as the baseline does.

The backdrop blur cost about 35% on both tiers: the panel re-blurred a city that is redrawn every frame. It is gone; the glass is darker instead (0.92), and the waiting mark's pulse animates opacity only. The final stills were retaken without the blur, and over the overview the text still holds (Review Focus 2). The Brief now passes the 10% gate at both tiers, and the city-alone rows have not moved.
